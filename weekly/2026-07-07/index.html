Schneider Electric · via Modern Executive Solutions / Gloat

89% of 150,000 employees using an AI talent marketplace — without a mandate

When internal surveys revealed that nearly 50% of exiting employees cited a lack of internal growth opportunities as their primary reason for leaving, Schneider Electric's Global CHRO Charise Le launched the Open Talent Market (OTM) in 2019 — an AI-powered platform built with Gloat that connects 150,000+ employees to full-time roles, gig projects, learning resources, and mentors across the company. A 20% time allocation policy let employees take on project work without manager permission.

Key design move: they removed the 3-year tenure requirement for internal role changes and stripped manager approval from gig and mentoring connections. Adoption followed organically. COVID-19 accelerated global rollout — the platform became the easiest way to find internal teammates during hiring freezes.

📈 89% employee adoption · 27,500 active mentor matches · 13,400 project/gig matches · 60% adoption within first month of launch

Vendor case study (Gloat promoting its platform) — but it names real people, cites verified adoption numbers, and the design logic is independently checkable. Source →

💡 L&D Lesson: Schneider's adoption rate didn't come from mandates — it came from removing friction. The platform worked because they changed policy first (no tenure lock-in, no manager approval gate) before launching the tool. The learning is structural, not technological: design the system so the right move is also the easiest move.
100-Developer Engineering Team · via Digital Applied

Structured AI enablement → 28% productivity lift sustained over 6 months

An anonymized 100-developer organization piloted GitHub Copilot with two groups: one got license access and a kickoff demo; the other got a structured 32-entry shared skill library, weekly "AI pair programming" sessions, and a defined ramp curve with checkpoints at weeks 2, 6, and 12. After six months, the structured group sustained a 28% productivity lift measured by PR merge velocity and cycle time.

The unstructured group plateaued at 9% gain by week 4 and saw engagement fall off — consistent with the broader finding from KORE1's analysis that developers require 11 weeks of supported practice to realize meaningful Copilot gains without a program, and many give up before then.

📈 28% productivity lift (structured) vs. 9% plateau (access-only) · Key variable: the shared skill library + scheduled practice, not the tool itself

Company name not disclosed in source. The pattern matches multiple published analyses of Copilot rollouts. Source →

💡 L&D Lesson: The variable that predicted outcome wasn't the tool — it was whether the team had a structured practice path. A shared skill library (what to learn, in what order, with what peer checkpoints) compressed 11 weeks of stumbling into 2-3 weeks of intentional ramp. That's a program design problem, not a technology problem.

From Content Factory to Proof Engine

What changed: RedThread Research's 2026 State of L&D report — tracking 461 vendors — found that Analytics & Measurement jumped from #3 to #1 priority in just three years, now cited by 83% of vendors (up from 33% in 2023). Content Creation, which dominated in 2021 and 2023, dropped to fifth. At the same time, AI adoption in learning tech is near-universal: only 2% of vendors report no AI usage, down from 24% three years ago.

The ATD picture: The 2026 State of the Industry report tells a complicated story. Average formal learning hours per employee rose to 16.7 in 2025 (up from 13.7 in 2024) — but direct expenditure per learner dropped sharply, from $1,254 to $846. More hours, less money. That's either a productivity gain or a quality squeeze — probably both.

The learner signal: Coursera's Job Skills Report 2026 reports 234% YoY growth in GenAI and agentic AI enrollments, with 14 enrollments per minute in GenAI content. Agentic AI (AI systems that take action on behalf of users) jumped to the #3 fastest-growing GenAI skill. The gap between what learners are chasing and what most L&D programs offer is widening fast.

Boye & Company · Jun 2026 · Survey of 1,300 engineers across 25 teams
AI Engineering Maturity: What 1,300 Engineers Actually Told Us About Working With AI
Counterintuitive: junior engineers scored higher on AI maturity than seniors. The reason matters for how you design onboarding.

A survey of 1,300 engineers across 25 engineering teams found that junior engineers (P1 band) had a median AI maturity score of 2.25, while senior engineers (P4 band) scored 1.82 — nearly half a level lower. Why? Senior engineers have deeply-ingrained solo problem-solving habits; juniors started coding in an AI-assisted world and never developed strong resistance to using AI. The implication: your highest-experienced engineers may need the most intentional enablement, not the least.

What this week means for Lemonade's product & engineering L&D 🍋

📏 1. The skill atlas you already own

The signalSchneider Electric's 89% adoption came not from a new tool but from removing friction — policy changes first, platform second. The data that powers their talent marketplace already existed in HR records; they just wired it differently.

The moveLemonade's engineering team leaves a behavioral trail every day: GitHub commits, code review patterns, Jira tickets, on-call assignments, PR review frequency. That trail is richer than any self-reported skills survey. Start treating it as your skills atlas. Who has actually shipped work near the payments service? Who has reviewed PRs in the underwriting codebase? That's a mobility signal, not a survey answer.

⏱️ 2. Compress the 11-week AI ramp

The signalWithout a structured program, developers take 11 weeks to realize meaningful Copilot productivity gains — and many give up before then. With a 32-item shared skill library and weekly checkpoints, teams compresses this to 2-3 weeks.

The moveLemonade's 3+ month engineering ramp is the right context for this intervention. Build a shared AI skill library for Lemonade's specific codebase context — not generic Copilot tips, but prompts, patterns, and anti-patterns that apply to Lemonade's architecture, claims flow, and underwriting logic. Add a week-2 and week-6 checkpoint conversation between the new hire and their buddy. Measure PR velocity at week 8.

📐 3. Define your measurement before the next program

The signalLess than a quarter of ATD respondents measure whether their training achieves its organizational goals. RedThread's entire market shift is driven by this gap — the industry is now selling the measurement it previously ignored.

The moveBefore launching the next L&D initiative at Lemonade — onboarding revamp, AI fluency program, knowledge management project — write down the behavioral change you expect to see at week 1, week 4, and week 12. Not completion rates. Behavioral change: "Engineers on AI-assisted teams review AI-generated code by checking X before merging." Then build the measurement before the content.
