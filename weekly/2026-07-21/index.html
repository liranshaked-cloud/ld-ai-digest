The Josh Bersin Company + Accenture · Workday Learning / Sana, GA Jul 22, 2026

Moving an entire learning library from static to AI-native: months, not years

When Josh Bersin's company shifted to Sana (now Workday Learning, powered by Sana), the challenge was the same one every large organization faces: a library of valuable but static PDFs, slide decks, and recorded courses that nobody was actually using. The conversion didn't take a year-long content redesign. Using Sana's AI-native authoring, thousands of learners moved into an interactive, always-on environment and a large library of programs was converted into interactive courses in months rather than years.

At Accenture, the rationale was similar but at a different scale: a global workforce that needed to continuously build capabilities without the sequential market-by-market delays of traditional LMS rollouts. Workday Learning's AI-powered auto-translation and automated assignment logic let the company run simultaneous global learning campaigns rather than staging them across 12-month deployment windows.

Platform-level outcomes reported at GA: 3x higher learner engagement vs. legacy LMS systems, 5x faster compliance reporting, and content creation timelines compressed from multi-week production cycles to hours. The core unlock is not the content format — it's that AI tutors can now provide in-the-moment, role-specific support that a static course cannot.

📈 Months not years · 3× engagement vs. legacy LMS · 5× faster compliance reporting · 98% faster content creation

Source: Workday newsroom, July 22, 2026 GA press release — customer quotes from Josh Bersin and Colin Anderson (Accenture COO, HR). Source →

💡 L&D Lesson: The biggest barrier to library modernization isn't budget — it's the assumption that every piece of content needs to be manually rebuilt. AI-native authoring flips that: existing material becomes the input, not the obstacle. If your organization is sitting on a library of valuable-but-inaccessible content, this is the architecture that changes the math.
TalentLMS Learning Debt Report · Jul 15, 2026 · Survey of 1,000+ employees

"Learning Debt": the skill gaps AI is hiding — and what happens when they surface

TalentLMS's July 2026 report introduces the concept of "Learning Debt" — unaddressed training needs that accumulate invisibly when AI tools allow employees to complete work they don't actually understand. The pattern: 59% of employees use AI to complete tasks they were never trained for. The AI delivers a plausible output; the employee submits it. The skill gap doesn't appear in any training record — but it shows up in work quality.

37% say AI makes them appear more competent than they actually are. 29% delivered work they couldn't explain if asked. 47% stayed silent about their skill gaps rather than flagging them to their manager. 28% of managers don't know how often their team members are struggling — because the AI output looks fine.

When the debt surfaces, the consequences are concrete: employees with significant learning debt are 6× more likely to make preventable mistakes. The top impacts reported: 65% cite work quality as the first thing that suffers, 50% point to productivity loss, and 48% say it weakens overall performance.

⚠️ 6× more preventable mistakes · 37% appear more competent than they are · 47% hide their gaps · 65% see work quality decline

Source: TalentLMS Learning Debt Report, published July 15, 2026. Exact sample size not disclosed in press release; methodology available from TalentLMS directly. Source →

💡 L&D Lesson: Learning debt is a silent risk, not a visible one. It doesn't show up in your LMS completion data. It doesn't show up in performance reviews until it's too late. The only way to surface it is to design for understanding — not just output. Code review rituals, explain-back questions, and explicit "how did you get here?" conversations are L&D interventions, not management overhead.

These two stories — more powerful platforms, more hidden gaps — aren't a contradiction. They're the same week's news because they're the same problem: AI capability has raced ahead of the L&D infrastructure designed to support it.

What this week means for Lemonade's product & engineering L&D 🍋

🧠 1. Run a learning debt audit before it finds you

The signalTalentLMS: 37% of employees appear more competent than they are. 28% of managers don't know how often their team is struggling. In a fast-moving engineering org, this gap looks like confidently-shipped code that nobody can fully explain.

The moveAdd one question to Lemonade's code review template: "Briefly explain the core logic of your most significant change — not what it does, but how it works." This isn't a test — it's a forcing function. Engineers who've built real understanding write one sentence. Engineers with learning debt get stuck. The delta is diagnostic data for L&D, not punishment for the developer. Pair it with a no-judgment flag: "I used AI here and want to go deeper" as an accepted review comment. Surface the debt before it accumulates.

🗺️ 2. Map the AI tools already in use before building the training

The signalGo1: 80% of IT leaders found AI/learning tools deployed without IT review. The shadow AI problem in L&D mirrors the shadow AI problem in engineering generally: people adopt what works, then tell no one.

The moveBefore designing any AI enablement program at Lemonade, run a tool census first: send a 5-question Typeform to Lemonade's engineering org asking what AI tools they actually use daily (not what's officially approved). Cross-reference with what's licensed. The gap between those two lists is your real curriculum. You can't train people to use tools well if you don't know which tools they're using. The inventory is the first deliverable, not the last.

⚙️ 3. When evaluating any L&D tool: ask if the AI is foundational or cosmetic

The signalWorkday/Sana's GA marks a real architecture shift. The difference between a bolt-on AI feature (a "generate with AI" button) and an AI-native architecture (AI tutor + skills data + content engine as the same system) is not a marketing distinction — it's an outcomes distinction. 3× engagement vs. legacy LMS is the result of one, not the other.

The moveThe next time Lemonade evaluates any L&D or enablement tool, add one mandatory question to the vendor evaluation: "Show us how your AI layer connects to real employee data (role, skills, previous completions). Not a demo — a live instance with real or realistic data." A bolt-on AI feature can't answer this. An AI-native architecture answers it in the first 60 seconds. This filters out the feature theater and surfaces the systems that can actually do what Workday/Sana is claiming.
